Anyone who wants to operate a radio set on board needs a radio operator's certificate. But which one is the right one: the UBI for inland waterway radio, or the SRC for maritime radio? The two German certificates are often confused, yet they cover different waters and different radio procedures. This article explains the differences and helps you find the right certificate for your cruising area.

## The basic idea: two certificates, two types of water

Roughly speaking, the difference comes down to this:

- **UBI (VHF radio operator's certificate for inland navigation, "UKW-Sprechfunkzeugnis Binnen"):** covers voice radio on **inland waterways**, meaning rivers, canals and inland lakes.
- **SRC (Short Range Certificate):** covers VHF **maritime radio** with DSC in the coastal area, the so-called sea area A1 (roughly the coverage range of coastal radio stations, in practice around 30 to 40 nautical miles).

Both certificates are **independent of the German recreational boating license (Sportbootführerschein, SBF)**. You can obtain the inland SBF or the [coastal SBF](/en/sbf-coastal/) without a radio certificate at all. But as soon as you actually operate a radio set on board, you need the matching radio operator's certificate as well. If you never operate your own radio set, you can technically do without one, but in busy waterways or when chartering larger boats, a radio certificate is almost always useful in practice, or required by the charter company.

## What is the UBI?

The **VHF radio operator's certificate for inland navigation (UBI)** authorizes you to operate and supervise a ship radio station on inland waterways. The exam is administered by the joint examination boards of the German Sailing Association (DSV) and the German Motor Yacht Association (DMYV).

**UBI exam structure:**

- **Theory:** a questionnaire with **22 multiple-choice questions** in **60 minutes**, passed with **17 correct answers**.
- **Practical part:** mandatory tasks such as setting the Automatic Transmitter Identification System (**ATIS**), knowledge of the equipment, and correctly handling distress, urgency and safety messages.
- **Minimum age:** 15.

Content-wise, the UBI covers traffic circles and local radio on inland waterways, VHF operating procedures, ATIS technology, and distress and safety traffic. The official question catalog contains 130 questions, which you can work through systematically, for example with the [UBI module in the Boatpass app](/en/ubi/).

## What is the SRC?

The **Short Range Certificate (SRC)** is the official authorization to take part in the worldwide maritime radio service (GMDSS) in the VHF band. It authorizes you to operate VHF maritime radio equipment including **DSC (Digital Selective Calling)** in the coastal area. The exam is administered by the examination boards of the DSV and the German Motor Yacht Association (DMYV).

**SRC exam structure:**

- **Theory:** a written questionnaire with **24 multiple-choice questions** in **30 minutes**, passed with **19 correct answers**.
- In addition, the **written recording of a distress, urgency or safety message**.
- **Practical part:** four tasks on a DSC radio set.
- An **English section**, in which distress calls are received and translated, or German texts are translated into English.
- **Minimum age:** 15.

Content-wise, the SRC covers VHF maritime radio and the GMDSS system in sea area A1, DSC procedures, distress, urgency and safety traffic, ship-to-ship and ship-to-shore communication, and internationally applicable radio regulations. The official question catalog contains 180 questions, which you can train with, for example, the [SRC module from Boatpass](/en/src/). The SRC is internationally recognized and valid indefinitely.

## ATIS and DSC: two key terms explained

Both exams revolve heavily around technical procedures that can sound cryptic at first:

- **ATIS (Automatic Transmitter Identification System):** a system used in inland navigation, where every radio set automatically transmits an identification code whenever it transmits. This way, every radio station can be clearly assigned to a vessel. Correctly setting the ATIS code is a fixed part of the practical UBI exam.
- **DSC (Digital Selective Calling):** the digital selective calling procedure used in maritime radio, which lets you call a specific radio station directly or trigger a digital distress alert with your position at the push of a button. Operating a DSC radio set is a central part of the practical SRC exam.

If you obtain both certificates, you end up learning two different technical systems: ATIS for inland navigation and DSC for maritime radio.

## Where does the border between inland and maritime radio run?

The border between inland waterways and maritime shipping routes is the same border that determines whether you need the inland SBF or the [coastal SBF](/en/sbf-coastal/). On the Elbe, for example, it does not run along the coastline but only starts above Hamburg; on the Trave it runs at the Hubbrücke in Lübeck. The exact boundaries for the most important rivers are collected in our article [SBF Validity: Which License Applies Where?](/en/blog/sbf-validity-which-license-where/). Roughly speaking: wherever the coastal SBF is required, the SRC is also the relevant radio certificate for operating a radio set, and wherever the inland SBF applies, the UBI is sufficient.

## UBI or SRC: what do I need for my cruising area?

The answer mainly depends on where you are boating:

- **Inland waters** (the Rhine, the Elbe above the maritime shipping route boundary, the Danube, the Moselle, Berlin's waterways, Lake Müritz and other inland lakes): the **UBI** is enough here.
- **Coastal waters** (the North Sea and Baltic Sea, maritime shipping routes such as the Elbe below Hamburg): here you need the **SRC** as soon as you operate a VHF maritime radio set.
- **Both:** if you want to operate a radio both on inland waters and along the coast, for example because you charter on the Baltic in summer and cruise on domestic rivers on weekends, you need **both certificates**.

Importantly: **the UBI and the SRC are not mutually creditable.** They cover different radio procedures, frequency bands and traffic circles, even though both are based on VHF. An SRC alone does not authorize inland radio operation, and conversely a UBI does not authorize maritime radio operation.

There is, however, one simplification: **holders of an existing maritime radio certificate (SRC or LRC) get a shortened UBI exam**, both in the theoretical and the practical part, since the fundamentals of voice radio operation have already been demonstrated.

## And what about the LRC?

For those who want to operate radio on the high seas, there is a third tier: the **Long Range Certificate (LRC)**. It authorizes worldwide maritime radio beyond VHF, including medium and short wave as well as satellite radio within the GMDSS framework, and thus also covers sea areas A2 to A4. For the vast majority of recreational boaters in coastal waters, however, the SRC is entirely sufficient; the LRC becomes relevant mainly for long passages far from the coast. ## Cost overview

The official exam fees differ slightly between the two certificates:

- **UBI:** the total fee is around **€108.71**, made up of admission, the theory and practical exam, and issuing the certificate. Retaking either the theory or the practical part costs €45.64.
- **SRC:** the official fee charged by the DSV/DMYV is currently around **€127.88** for admission, the exam and issuing the certificate. An optional preparation course at a radio school typically costs a few hundred euros on top, but is not mandatory.

For both certificates, a preparation course is **not required**. The official question catalogs are freely available, so you can prepare independently with a suitable learning app such as Boatpass, without an expensive in-person course.

## Do I even need a UBI or SRC?

You only need a radio operator's certificate if you actually operate a maritime or inland radio station **yourself**. If you never use a radio set of your own, you technically don't need one. In practice, though, things look different: on busy inland waterways such as the Rhine or the Elbe, radio is extremely useful for communicating with commercial shipping traffic, and many charter boats, especially along the coast, come standard with a VHF maritime radio set including DSC. Many charter companies therefore require a valid SRC from skippers before handing over a yacht equipped with radio gear. A radio certificate is also a major advantage in an emergency, so you can correctly issue a DSC alert and a Mayday call.

It is also worth distinguishing between issuing an actual **distress call** and regular radio operation: anyone may issue a distress call in an acute emergency, even without a radio certificate, because in a genuine emergency, getting help quickly matters more than the formal authorization. For normal, everyday radio traffic outside of acute emergencies, however, you do need the appropriate certificate.

## Conclusion

The UBI and the SRC are two different radio operator's certificates for two different types of water: the **UBI** covers inland radio on rivers, canals and inland lakes, while the **SRC** covers VHF maritime radio with DSC in the coastal area. Both are independent of the SBF and not mutually creditable, though an existing SRC or LRC shortens the UBI exam. Once you know which waters you'll be boating in, you also know which certificate you need, and if you cover both types of water, there's no way around taking both exams.
