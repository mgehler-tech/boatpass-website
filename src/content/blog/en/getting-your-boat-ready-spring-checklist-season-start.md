After months in winter storage, most boat owners want to cast off the moment the first warm days arrive. But launching a boat without checking it over first risks unpleasant surprises out on the water, from a weak battery to a leak that developed unnoticed during the off-season. This article gives you a practical checklist so your boat starts the new season safely and reliably.

## Why a systematic spring check pays off

A boat that's sat idle for months changes during that time, even if it was carefully winterized. Seals can become brittle, moisture can build up unnoticed in enclosed spaces, and small animals often look for shelter in cushions or wiring over winter. A structured check before the first launch catches these problems while you're still ashore and have time to fix them, instead of discovering them out on the water. If you followed our [winterizing checklist](/en/blog/winterizing-a-boat-checklist-for-winter-storage/) carefully in autumn, spring usually means less work, but it's still worth going through every point.

## Checking the hull and underwater areas

Before the boat goes back in the water, it's worth giving the hull a thorough look:

- **Check the hull for cracks, blisters, or damage.** Osmosis blisters, cracks in the gelcoat, or damage to the underwater hull should be identified, and repaired professionally if needed, before launching.
- **Check the antifouling and refresh it if necessary.** Whether a touch-up or a full repaint is needed depends on the condition of the coating and how long it's been since the last application. When in doubt, a workshop or the antifouling manufacturer can advise.
- **Check seacocks, through-hulls, and hoses.** Every opening below the waterline, along with its hoses and clamps, should be checked for cracks, brittleness, and a secure fit before the boat goes back in the water.
- **Check sacrificial anodes on the hull.** As with an outboard, a heavily worn anode no longer protects reliably against corrosion and should be replaced in time. You'll find more on this in our article on [outboard motor basic maintenance](/en/blog/outboard-motor-basic-maintenance/).
- **Check the propeller and rudder assembly.** A look at the propeller blades, shaft, and rudder reveals any damage that may have occurred during storage or transport.

## Getting the engine ready

The engine needs particular attention after winter storage before it's ready for regular use again:

- **Check the oil level and condition.** If an oil change was done before storage, a visual check is usually enough. If the oil looks milky or the level is low, resolve that before the first start.
- **Check the spark plugs.** If the cylinders were treated with fogging oil in autumn, refit the spark plugs and check their condition before the first start, as described in our article on [outboard motor basic maintenance](/en/blog/outboard-motor-basic-maintenance/).
- **Check the fuel system.** Fuel that sat in the tank over winter can lose quality. Filters and lines should be checked and replaced if needed, especially if no stabilizer was used.
- **Get the cooling system ready again.** If the cooling system was filled with antifreeze, it needs to be removed or flushed according to the manufacturer's instructions before the first trip. When starting the engine, check that the cooling water stream is visibly flowing.
- **Take the first start-up carefully.** After a long idle period, start the engine with an external water supply or with the boat already in the water, never dry, and pay attention to unusual noises or smells.

For more complex inboard engines, or if you're unsure about any of the steps, a qualified workshop is worth consulting before the first trip of the season.

## Installing and checking the battery

If you removed or disconnected the battery over winter, keep a few points in mind before reinstalling it:

- **Check the charge level.** Even with a trickle charger connected, it's worth confirming the battery is actually fully charged before reconnecting it to the boat's electrical system.
- **Clean the terminals and connections.** Corrosion on the terminals leads to poor contact and can cause starting problems or electronics failures.
- **Check that it's securely mounted.** A loose battery can shift in rough water, get damaged, or cause a short circuit.
- **Check the wiring if you have multiple batteries.** After a long period of storage, it's also worth checking that the main switch and fuses are working correctly.

## Water system, bilge, and interior

It's not just the engine and hull, the inside of the boat deserves a check after winter too:

- **Flush the fresh water system.** If potable-water antifreeze was added in autumn, the system needs to be flushed thoroughly with clean water before first use.
- **Check the bilge and test the bilge pump.** Confirm the bilge is dry and that the pump works reliably in both automatic and manual mode. This is one of the most important safety checks before launching.
- **Check cushions, covers, and trim for moisture and mildew.** If moisture got trapped during storage, affected areas should be dried and cleaned before the season starts.
- **Check seals on hatches and windows.** Brittle or porous seals let rain in during summer and should be replaced in time.
- **Look for signs of rodents.** If mice or rats had access to the boat over winter, wiring or insulation may be damaged. Check any suspicious spots more closely before the first trip.

## Refreshing safety gear

Before heading out on the water, all safety equipment belongs on the checklist:

- **Check life jackets.** Check condition, buckles, and, for inflatable models, the trigger mechanism and CO2 cartridge if applicable. You'll find more on buoyancy classes and choosing the right life jacket in our article on [life jacket buoyancy classes](/en/blog/life-jacket-buoyancy-classes/).
- **Check distress signals for expiration dates.** Pyrotechnic distress signals have an expiration date and need to be replaced with current ones in time. More details are in our article on the [pyrotechnics certificate (FKN)](/en/blog/fkn-pyrotechnic-distress-signals/).
- **Check the first aid kit.** Check that bandages and any medication on board are complete and within their shelf life. What belongs on board is described in our article on [first aid on board](/en/blog/first-aid-on-board/).
- **Have a fire extinguisher checked, if you carry one.** Look for a valid inspection seal and correct fill pressure.
- **Test the flashlight, signal whistle, and other small gear.** Even small, often-overlooked items should be in working order before they're needed in an emergency.

## Checking electronics, navigation, and radio

Modern boats increasingly rely on electronics that should be tested after winter storage:

- **Start and update the GPS and chartplotter.** Check whether the chart data and software are up to date and whether the device reliably gets a signal.
- **Test the radio.** A quick functional test, ideally with a second station or after checking with the relevant coast station, shows whether transmit and receive functions are working properly.
- **Check navigation lights and other lighting.** All required lights should be working before the first night trip of the season.
- **Check the general electrical system.** A quick test of every consumer, from instrument lighting to the bilge pump, catches loose connections or blown fuses early.

## Don't forget paperwork, registration, and insurance

Alongside the technical checks, a few administrative points belong to the season start:

- **Check boat papers and registration.** Confirm all required documents are on board and still valid.
- **Review your insurance.** It's worth a look at your existing coverage, especially if anything about the boat or how you use it has changed. Our article on [boat insurance](/en/blog/boat-insurance-for-sportboats-what-makes-sense/) covers what generally matters here.
- **Sort out your slip and launch date early.** Check early with your marina or club about when your spot is available again and when the boat can be launched.

## The first outing of the season

Even once every point on the checklist is done, approach the first trip of the season with a bit of caution:

- **Choose calm, familiar waters.** A short test run in known waters is better suited than heading straight into a demanding trip in uncertain conditions.
- **Test the engine under load.** Only actual operation reliably shows whether the engine, cooling, and drive work together properly.
- **Check all systems under real conditions.** Steering, trim, navigation, and safety gear should be deliberately tested during the shakedown run, not for the first time when they're actually needed.
- **Check the forecast beforehand.** Calm, predictable weather suits the first outing of the season particularly well. Our article on the [marine weather report](/en/blog/marine-weather-report-warnings-weather-services-for-skippers/) explains how to check it properly.

## Common mistakes at the start of the season

A few mistakes show up again and again during the spring check:

- **Not leaving enough time.** Starting the check only shortly before your desired launch date leaves little room if unexpected problems come up.
- **Overlooking the bilge and bilge pump.** This safety-critical point is often neglected even though it's simple to check.
- **Only glancing at the safety gear.** Expired distress signals or a damaged life jacket are often only noticed when they're actually needed.
- **Heading straight into a longer trip without a test run.** Minor issues usually show up first on a short, low-key shakedown run, not far out on the water.
- **Not using the notes from autumn.** If you took notes or photos of the boat's condition while winterizing, use them for comparison during the spring check.

## The spring check and your SBF preparation

Checking over your own boat isn't part of the theoretical Sportbootführerschein (SBF) exam, but for most owners it's just as much a part of everyday boating life as [winterizing in autumn](/en/blog/winterizing-a-boat-checklist-for-winter-storage/) or [trailering and launching](/en/blog/trailering-and-launching-a-boat/). If you own a boat, it pays to plan realistically for the ongoing costs of maintenance, mooring, and gear from the start, our [full cost overview for the Sportbootführerschein](/en/sbf-costs/) gives you a starting point.

For the theoretical exam itself, targeted practice with the official question catalog remains the most important building block. With the [Boatpass app](/en/sbf-inland-exam-questions/) you can study the original [SBF Inland](/en/sbf-inland/) and [SBF Coastal](/en/sbf-coastal/) exam questions and test yourself in exam mode to see how ready you are, before turning your attention to getting your own boat ready for the season.

## Conclusion

A systematic spring check is the best preparation for a relaxed and safe season. Work through the hull, engine, battery, water systems, safety gear, and electronics in order, and treat the first outing deliberately as a shakedown run, and you'll avoid most unpleasant surprises on the water. Give the season start the time it needs, and when a step feels beyond you, a qualified workshop can help with the more technical parts, then nothing stands in the way of a great new season.
