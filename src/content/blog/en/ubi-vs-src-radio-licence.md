If you are equipping a boat in Germany or preparing for the German recreational boat licence, you will soon come across two abbreviations: **UBI** and **SRC**. Both are radio operator's certificates that authorise you to use a VHF ship radio station – but they apply to completely different waterways. Which one you need depends entirely on where you sail.

## Why Do You Need a Radio Certificate at All?

A ship's radio is not optional equipment for most practical purposes. On German inland waterways and at sea, a VHF radio – whether built in or handheld – is the standard tool for calling locks, announcing your passage to bridge operators, coordinating marina entry, and raising the emergency services in a distress situation.

The key legal point: operating a ship radio station in Germany requires a valid radio operator's certificate. Using a VHF radio on a waterway without the appropriate certificate is a regulatory offence. The **only exception** applies in genuine distress – in a life-threatening emergency, anyone may send a MAYDAY call or activate a digital distress alert regardless of whether they hold a certificate. This right is expressly protected so that no one hesitates to call for help on a procedural technicality.

The authority responsible for issuing both certificates in Germany is the **Bundesnetzagentur** (Federal Network Agency).

## The UBI – Radio Certificate for Inland Waterways

The **UBI** (radio operator's certificate for inland waterway radio, in German: Funkzeugnis für den Binnenschifffahrtsfunk) authorises you to operate a ship radio station on the German inland waterway network – rivers, canals, and inland lakes that fall under inland shipping regulations.

### When Do You Need the UBI?

You need the UBI if you want to use a VHF radio (installed or handheld, operated as a ship's station) on **German federal inland waterways** – the Rhine, Danube, Elbe, Moselle, Kiel Canal (Nord-Ostsee-Kanal), and comparable waterways.

In practice this means: calling locks by radio, contacting bridge control centres or traffic management stations, or taking part in the general inland waterway radio service all require the UBI. If you are working towards or already hold the **SBF Binnen** (German inland recreational boat licence), adding the UBI is a logical next step – the subjects overlap and most sailing schools bundle them together.

### What Does the UBI Cover?

Inland waterway radio uses its own frequency allocation and channel assignments. **Channel 10** is the widely used calling channel for recreational craft on many stretches of inland waterway; specific channels for locks, bridge operators, and traffic control centres vary by waterway.

UBI training covers:
- Channel assignments in the inland waterway radio service
- Calling and working procedures (locks, bridges, traffic centres)
- Priority traffic and emergency communication on inland waters
- Legal requirements for inland radio operation

### The UBI Examination

The UBI exam includes a **written component** (questions on regulations and operating procedures) and a **practical component** (simulated radio calls). Most candidates prepare in a half-day to full-day course. Accredited sailing schools and boating clubs across Germany offer UBI preparation, often bundled with the SBF Binnen. After passing, the Bundesnetzagentur issues the certificate.

## The SRC – Radio Certificate for Coastal and Offshore Waters

The **SRC (Short Range Certificate)** is the internationally recognised radio operator's certificate for maritime VHF operation at sea, defined under the European CEPT standard. It authorises you to operate a ship radio station on coastal shipping lanes and on the open sea, and it is valid across CEPT member countries – covering most of Europe.

### When Do You Need the SRC?

You need the SRC if you want to use a marine VHF radio station (installed or handheld, operated as a ship's station) on **coastal shipping lanes or international waters** – the North Sea and Baltic Sea, German coastal zones, and any international sea passage.

The SRC is closely linked to the [SBF See](https://boatpass.de/sbf-see/) (German coastal recreational boat licence). Many sailing schools offer both the SBF See course and SRC preparation together, since the topics complement each other: collision avoidance rules, distress signals, and radio procedures all overlap in meaningful ways.

### What Does the SRC Cover?

Maritime VHF radio operates under different rules from inland waterway radio. The SRC covers:

- **DSC (Digital Selective Calling):** The digital distress alerting system built into modern marine VHF radios. When triggered, a MAYDAY DSC alert automatically transmits the vessel's MMSI number and – if a GPS is connected – its current position. **Channel 70** is the dedicated DSC channel and is used exclusively for digital calls; it is never used for voice communication.
- **MAYDAY, PAN-PAN, SÉCURITÉ:** The three priority levels in maritime radio. MAYDAY signals a life-threatening emergency. PAN-PAN signals urgency without immediate threat to life – for example, a mechanical breakdown that has not yet become a distress situation. SÉCURITÉ signals a safety announcement such as a navigational hazard or a weather alert. The correct wording and procedure for each call is examined.
- **Channel 16:** The international distress and safety calling channel, permanently monitored by coastguard stations and rescue services in coastal areas.
- **MMSI (Maritime Mobile Service Identity):** The nine-digit identification number registered with the **BSH (Bundesamt für Seeschifffahrt und Hydrographie – Federal Maritime and Hydrographic Agency)** for each maritime radio installation. It is transmitted automatically with every DSC call and allows rescue coordination centres to identify the vessel immediately.
- General operating procedures, frequency allocations, and voice communication at sea

### The SRC Examination

The SRC exam consists of a **written component** and an **oral/practical component** with simulated radio scenarios. Typical tasks include composing and transmitting a MAYDAY call correctly, operating a DSC controller, and handling priority message sequences in the right order. Preparation typically takes one to one-and-a-half days. Accredited sailing schools, boating clubs, and specialist radio training providers offer courses throughout Germany. The certificate is issued by the Bundesnetzagentur.

## UBI or SRC – What Do You Actually Need?

The decision depends on your sailing area and the radio equipment on board:

**You sail exclusively on inland waterways** (canals, rivers, inland lakes under inland shipping regulations): You need the **UBI** only.

**You sail exclusively at sea** (coastal waters, open sea, international passages): You need the **SRC** only.

**You sail both inland and at sea** (for example, passages along the Elbe from Hamburg out into the North Sea): You need **both certificates**, since each is valid only in its respective domain.

**You have no radio on board:** No certificate is required – but you cannot legally conduct any radio traffic. On routes with locks or in harbours where radio coordination is expected, that is a real practical constraint.

**A note on Lake Constance (Bodensee):** The Bodensee is a border lake shared between Germany, Austria, and Switzerland. The German portion falls under inland waterway law, which makes the UBI the relevant certificate for German waters. The Austrian and Swiss portions are governed by those countries' own regulations; anyone planning to sail the whole lake should check the rules for all three jurisdictions.

## Registration and Validity

Both certificates are issued by the **Bundesnetzagentur** after an exam conducted by an accredited examination centre. Many sailing schools with the appropriate accreditation run the exam on-site. Once issued, the certificates are generally **valid for life** – there is no periodic renewal requirement.

If you are preparing for the [SBF See](https://boatpass.de/sbf-see/) or [SBF Binnen](https://boatpass.de/sbf-binnen/) at the same time, many schools offer combined packages that cover the boat licence and the matching radio certificate in one course, saving time and often cost as well.

## Radio Knowledge in the SBF Exam (ELWIS)

Maritime radio topics appear in the official ELWIS exam catalogue that forms the basis for both SBF examinations. Typical questions include:

- Which channel is the international distress and calling channel at sea?
- What is DSC (Digital Selective Calling) and what happens when the distress button is activated?
- What is the correct wording of a MAYDAY call?
- What is the MMSI and what is it used for?
- What is the difference between a MAYDAY and a PAN-PAN call?

Basic radio knowledge is therefore part of the boat licence exam itself – even though the radio certificate is obtained separately. For a broader look at what catches candidates off guard in the SBF exam, see our article on the [most common SBF exam mistakes](/blog/en/most-common-sbf-mistakes/).

## Conclusion

UBI and SRC are both mandatory – but for different waterways. If you sail inland, you need the **UBI**; if you sail at sea, you need the **SRC**; and if you plan to do both, you need both. In either case the exam is very manageable with focused preparation, and most sailing schools offer the radio certificate as a natural companion to the **Sportbootführerschein** (SBF).
