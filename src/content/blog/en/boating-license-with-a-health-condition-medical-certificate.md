You want to get the German recreational boating license (the **Sportbootführerschein**, or SBF) but live with a health condition, and you wonder whether that is possible at all. The good news first: the medical certificate is not a "healthy or out" test. It is an **assessment of whether you can operate a recreational boat safely**. This article explains what is behind the certificate, what role aids and conditions play, and how to prepare well.

## What the medical certificate confirms

To be admitted to the exam, the examination committee requires the **medical certificate for boating license applicants**. You can get the form from the DMYV or DSV. It confirms your physical and health suitability and specifically covers **vision**, **color discrimination** and **hearing**. All formal requirements are covered in [Boating License Requirements](/en/blog/boating-license-requirements/), and the details of the vision test in [Eyesight Test for the German Boating License](/en/blog/eyesight-test-boating-license-requirements-process/).

What matters here: the certificate is a **medical judgment in each individual case**. The doctor records the results and confirms whether you are suitable in their view. A visit to your GP is enough, a specialist is not required. The certificate is valid for 12 months, and costs usually range from 30 to 65 euros depending on the practice.

## Suitable does not mean "perfectly healthy"

Many people shy away even from the appointment because they assume any diagnosis is a deal breaker. That is not the idea. The decisive question is whether you meet the demands of operating a recreational boat: you need to recognize lights, buoys and other vessels, perceive sound signals and calls, and react reliably in critical situations.

Whether a particular condition stands in the way depends heavily on the **individual case**: how pronounced it is, whether it is well treated, and whether aids make the difference. That is why this article deliberately does not offer a blanket "allowed or forbidden" list for individual diagnoses. Anyone who claims to have one owes you the proof. The assessment of your doctor is what counts.

## Aids and conditions

Many limitations can be compensated with aids:

- **Visual aids:** Glasses or contact lenses are allowed. The value with your usual visual aid counts.
- **Hearing aids:** If your hearing is only sufficient with a hearing aid, that is also permitted and noted on the certificate.
- **Conditions on the certificate:** If your suitability is only established with an aid, it is written on the certificate. In practice that means you wear the aid while operating the boat.

A practical tip from life on board: carry a **spare**. A second pair of glasses or spare batteries for the hearing aid take up no room and save you from an unpleasant situation, for example if your glasses go overboard.

## Vision, color vision, hearing: the three tested areas

Since the certificate covers exactly these areas, an honest look is worthwhile.

### Vision and color vision

The minimum visual acuity and the handling of red-green color deficiency are described in detail in the vision test article. In short: poor eyesight or a color deficiency does not automatically rule you out, the doctor assesses it case by case. Color plays a big role for lights and buoyage, more in [Navigation Lights Explained](/en/blog/navigation-lights-explained/).

### Hearing

On the water, **sound signals** are an essential part of communication, for example in fog or during maneuvers. Good to know: you are also admitted with a hearing aid, provided it brings your hearing to a sufficient level. The signals themselves are covered in [Sound Signals for the SBF Explained](/en/blog/sound-signals-sbf-explained/).

### General physical fitness

Beyond the three individual areas, the doctor confirms your general suitability. There are no rigid templates here, but an overall medical assessment. Complaints that could affect you while mooring, in an emergency or during maneuvers are exactly what you should discuss with the doctor.

## How to prepare for the doctor's appointment

The better the doctor knows your situation, the more reliable the assessment. This helps:

1. **Speak openly:** Explain that you need the certificate to operate a recreational boat and describe what you have to do on the water.
2. **Bring findings:** Current documents from your treating doctors make the assessment easier.
3. **Bring your aids:** Glasses, contact lenses or hearing aid belong at the appointment.
4. **Ask questions:** If a condition is noted, have them explain what it means in practice.
5. **Choose the timing:** Do not have the certificate issued too early, it is valid for only 12 months. Two to three months before the exam date is common.

## If the certificate is restricted or refused

It can happen that the doctor has concerns. That does not have to be the end.

- **Ask:** Have them explain concretely what the issue is and whether an aid, an adjustment of treatment or a condition would change the picture.
- **Second opinion:** You can obtain another medical assessment, for example from a specialist for your condition.
- **Ask the examination committee:** If you are unsure how the procedure works in your case or what the committee accepts, the responsible examination committee is the right contact. Ask **before** registering and before paying fees. How registration works is explained in [How to Register for the SBF Exam](/en/blog/how-to-register-sbf-exam/).

Honesty pays off: a certificate that lists a condition is better than a surprise on the water. This is about your safety and that of your passengers.

## The certificate is a snapshot

The certificate confirms your suitability at the time of the examination. After that, **you** are responsible for being fit enough each time you operate a boat. That concerns very practical points:

- **Medication:** Some medicines cause drowsiness or slow your reactions. Read the leaflet and, if in doubt, ask your doctor or pharmacist whether you should operate a boat on them.
- **Alcohol:** The rules and limits are covered in [Blood Alcohol Limit on a Boat](/en/blog/blood-alcohol-limit-boat/).
- **Changes:** If your vision or hearing gets worse, have it checked and adjust your aids, even if your certificate is formally still valid.
- **How you feel today:** If you are exhausted, ill or have low blood sugar, it is not a good day to skipper.

## Practical adjustments for everyday life on board

Even with a limitation, a lot can be arranged safely. A few ideas that need no special rules:

- **Brief your crew:** Show at least one other person how the engine, radio and rescue equipment work and what to do in an emergency. For the maneuver, see [Man Overboard Maneuver](/en/blog/man-overboard-maneuver/).
- **Wear a life jacket:** A suitable vest adds safety, more in [Life Jacket Buoyancy Classes](/en/blog/life-jacket-buoyancy-classes/).
- **Drive calmly and plan ahead:** Careful planning of area, weather and the day's schedule gives you more reserves.
- **Keep gear within reach:** Stow important items so you can reach them without contortions.

## Exam and preparation

The theory part of the exam consists of questions from the official ELWIS catalog, which you can study calmly and at your own pace. That is an advantage especially if you are managing a limitation: you choose your study times and repeat as often as you need. Everything about the exam procedure is on the [exam procedure page](/en/sbf-exam/). With the **Boatpass app** you practice the official questions in exam mode, at home or on the go, in small sessions and with direct feedback on where you stand.

## Conclusion

The medical certificate for the boating license is not a hurdle only the healthy can clear, but an individual assessment of whether you can operate a boat safely. Visual and hearing aids are expressly permitted, and conditions are noted on the certificate. Talk openly with your doctor, bring findings and aids, and clarify uncertainties with the examination committee in good time. After that, be honest with yourself, keep spares on board and only skipper when you feel fit. Then nothing stands between you and your [SBF Inland](/en/sbf-inland/) or [SBF Coastal](/en/sbf-coastal/).
