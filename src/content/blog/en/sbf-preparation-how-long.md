One of the most common questions before starting SBF prep: how long do I actually need to study? The honest answer depends on your prior knowledge and daily consistency. Here are realistic estimates – without sugarcoating.

## Short answer: 3–6 weeks at 30 minutes daily

This applies to **SBF Inland (Binnen) for motor vessels** – the most common license. Studying 30–45 minutes daily, most candidates are exam-ready in 3–6 weeks. Fast learners with technical background can be ready in 2–3 weeks; casual learners may need 6–8 weeks.

> Note: This is for **theory only**. The practical exam (driving a boat on the water) requires separate on-water practice sessions – no app can substitute for that.

## How much material is it, really?

The time estimate makes more sense once you know the scope. The official question catalog for SBF Inland for motor vessels contains **253 questions**: 72 basic questions (Basisfragen) and 181 inland-specific questions. In the exam you get a sheet with **30 questions** (7 basic and 23 specific), and you have **45 minutes** to complete it.

You pass with at least **5 of 7** basic questions and at least **18 of 23** specific questions correct. The two blocks are scored separately, so you cannot make up for gaps in one part with the other. The full exam-day procedure is described on our [SBF exam](/en/sbf-exam/) page.

For your planning this means: every question is known in advance, there are no surprises. With around 15 hours of study time you have a good three and a half minutes per question on average, repetition included. That is enough if you do not spread the time evenly but put it into the questions you get wrong.

## Study plans by learning type

### Fast learner – 2–3 weeks

**Prerequisites:** Technical background (e.g., a sailing course, time on the water, a technical profession), good concentration.

| Week | Focus | Daily time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cover all categories once, secure base questions | 45–60 min |
| 2 | Target weak spots, first full mock exams | 45–60 min |
| 3 (optional) | Exam simulations, daily knot practice | 30 min |

**Tip:** Start with the hardest categories (light signals, sound signals, right-of-way rules) and leave the easier ones (safety equipment, basic engine knowledge) for last.

### Average learner – 4–5 weeks

**Prerequisites:** No prior knowledge needed, 30 minutes daily is realistic.

| Week | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1 | Waterway rules and navigation signs (largest content volume) |
| 2 | Light signals and sound signals (hardest category) |
| 3 | Seamanship and engine knowledge |
| 4 | Weather and safety |
| 5 | Mock exams, close remaining gaps |

### Casual learner – 6–8 weeks

**Prerequisites:** Studies 3–4 times per week, daily time not always available.

Spread the above plan across 6–8 weeks. The key: study **at least 3 times per week**. Longer gaps than 3–4 days cause significant forgetting of recent material.

## What takes the most time?

From experience, these are the three most time-consuming areas. The percentages are rough rules of thumb, not official figures:

### 1. Light signals (~30–40% of study time)

Which vessel shows which light, in which situation? The combinations are numerous: motorboats underway, anchored vessels, sailboats, tugboats, fishing vessels – each has different requirements. Add night and reduced-visibility scenarios.

**Tip:** Study [light signals](/en/blog/navigation-lights-explained/) with visual diagrams, not text descriptions. The illustrations in the official question catalog are unambiguous – visualize each scenario.

### 2. Right-of-way rules (~20–30% of study time)

Who gives way to whom? When do you have right of way? The rules seem logical but exam questions specifically target edge cases: narrow channels, overtaking situations, encountering commercial shipping. Our article on [right-of-way and collision rules](/en/blog/right-of-way-collision-rules/) gives an overview with memory aids.

### 3. Seamanship and knots (practical)

[Knots](/en/blog/most-important-knots-sbf/) cannot be learned by reading – only by physical practice. Buy a 1-meter piece of rope and put it on your desk. **Five minutes daily** is enough to reliably master the bowline, clove hitch, and figure-eight knot. In the practical exam, seven knots are requested from a list of nine, and you must tie and explain at least six of them correctly.

## Why daily practice beats long sessions

The brain consolidates new information primarily **during sleep**. Someone who studies four hours on the weekend benefits less than someone who studies 30 minutes daily – because the daily schedule gives the brain multiple sleep cycles to process the material.

**Concretely:** 30 minutes daily × 30 days = 15 hours of study. Spread across 5 weekends at 3 hours each = also 15 hours – but with significantly lower retention.

## SBF Coastal or both licenses: how the timeline shifts

The weekly plans above apply to SBF Inland. For [SBF Coastal (See)](/en/sbf-coastal/) you should plan a bit more time, for two reasons:

- **More questions:** The official catalog contains 285 questions (the same 72 basic questions plus 213 coastal-specific ones).
- **Chart work:** On top of the question sheet you solve a navigation task with 9 questions, at least 7 of which must be correct. The catalog contains 15 chart tasks for this. Converting courses, plotting bearings and measuring distances can only be learned by practicing with chart, course plotter and dividers, not by reading.

As a rule of thumb, plan **one to two extra weeks** for the navigation tasks and solve every chart task completely at least once. How the tasks are structured is covered in [SBF Coastal navigation tasks explained](/en/blog/sbf-coastal-navigation-tasks-explained/).

If you take **both licenses**, you save study time: the 72 basic questions are identical, so you only learn them once. If you already hold one of the two licenses, you do not have to answer the basic questions again in the second exam. Which order makes sense for you is explained in our comparison [SBF Inland vs. Coastal](/en/blog/sbf-inland-vs-coastal/).

## How to tell you are exam-ready

The number of weeks is only a guideline. These three criteria are more reliable:

1. **You have seen every question in the catalog at least once.** Otherwise gaps in individual topics only show up in the exam.
2. **You pass several mock exams in a row with a margin.** Work with the real limits: you can afford at most 2 mistakes in the basic part and at most 5 in the specific part. If you regularly only just pass the simulation, a bit of exam-day nerves can push you below the line.
3. **You finish the sheet well under 45 minutes.** That leaves time in the real exam to double-check answers you are unsure about.

The practical part works differently: what counts there is time on the water. Plan at least one practice day on the boat and practice your knots alongside the theory, so both are solid by exam day.

## When to book your exam slot

Book your exam date **before** you finish your study plan. A concrete deadline motivates and prevents endless postponement. Slots with the DMYV and DSV examination boards can fill up early in spring and summer, so look for free dates a few weeks ahead. Which documents you need is covered in [how to register for the SBF exam](/en/blog/how-to-register-sbf-exam/).

**Recommendation:** Register when you've covered roughly 60–70% of the material. The exam date will motivate you to complete the remaining 30–40%.

**Good to know:** Theory and practice are separate exam parts and can be taken on different dates. Under the German recreational boating license regulation (Sportbootführerscheinverordnung), however, all parts must be passed **within one year**, counted from the first exam part you sit. So do not put off the second part for too long. What applies if one part does not work out is explained in [failed the SBF Inland exam: what to do next](/en/blog/failed-sbf-inland-exam-what-to-do-next/).

## Summary

For [SBF Inland](/en/sbf-inland/), **4–5 weeks** at 30 minutes daily is realistic for most candidates without prior knowledge. For SBF Coastal, add one to two weeks for chart work. The key is consistency, not intensity, and you are exam-ready once you repeatedly pass mock exams with a margin.
