The German federal government is planning the biggest change to the country's recreational boating license system in decades. Under the new Sportschifffahrtsverordnung (recreational boating regulation), the official Sportbootführerschein will be replaced by certificates issued by recognized water sports associations. Here's what this means for you, whether your existing license stays valid and what's changing for electric motors.

## What exactly is changing?

The core of the reform: the official Sportbootführerschein Binnen (inland) and See (coastal) will be discontinued in their current form. Instead of government bodies, **recognized water sports associations** such as the Deutscher Motoryachtverband (DMYV) and the Deutscher Segler-Verband (DSV) will issue the new certificates.

These new documents are called **Verbandsscheine** (association certificates). They are no longer "official" in the traditional government-issued sense but are issued independently by authorized associations and printed by the Bundesdruckerei (Federal Printing Office). Each Verbandsschein simultaneously serves as an **International Certificate of Competence (ICC)** under UN/ECE Resolution No. 40.

**Important:** The training and examination content remains the same. Whether the certificate is called "amtlich" (official) or "Verbandsschein" makes no difference to what you need to learn and demonstrate for the [SBF exam](/en/sbf-exam/).

## Timeline and transition periods

The Federal Ministry of Transport (BMV) originally intended for the new regulation to enter into force at the beginning of the 2026 boating season. That date has since passed: the regulation remains under legal review – among other things over the question of whether a sovereign task like issuing licenses can be delegated to private associations without formal authorization (Beleihung). No new binding date has been set as of August 2026; the transition period for the licensing system is currently targeted for around 01.01.2028.

In practical terms, right now:

- **For now:** The official SBF remains fully valid and continues to be issued exactly as before.
- **Still open:** When and in what form the Verbandsscheine will actually be introduced is not yet settled – DMYV and DSV themselves are still waiting on an updated timeline from the ministry.

## Does my existing license remain valid?

**Yes, without restriction.** All previously issued official Sportbootführerscheine retain their **unlimited validity**. You do not need to exchange or renew your license. This also applies to older licenses issued before the 2017 Sportbootführerscheinverordnung.

If you are currently working on your [SBF Inland](/en/sbf-inland/) or [SBF Coastal](/en/sbf-coastal/), the reform changes nothing about the validity of your certificate.

## New rules for electric motors

Alongside the switch to association certificates, the Sportschifffahrtsverordnung introduces another significant change: the **power threshold for the license requirement** is being standardized.

Previously:

- **Combustion engine:** License required above 11.03 kW (15 HP)
- **Electric motor:** License required above 7.5 kW (approx. 10 HP)

Going forward, **one uniform limit applies to all drive types:** License required above **11.03 kW (15 HP)**.

This means: if you operate an electric boat with a power output between 7.5 kW and 11.03 kW, you will **no longer need a license**. For all boats above 15 HP, the requirement remains regardless of whether it's a combustion engine or electric motor.

## Why the reform?

The BMV is pursuing several goals with the new regulation:

- **Reducing bureaucracy:** Leaner processes as the associations take over issuance independently.
- **Standardization:** Aligning the different power thresholds for combustion and electric motors.
- **International compatibility:** The Verbandsschein simultaneously serves as an ICC, simplifying boating abroad.

## Criticism of the reform

The reform has met broad criticism from the water sports community. Both the DMYV and the DSV have publicly spoken out against the planned regulation.

The **main points of criticism:**

- The loss of official government status could make **international recognition** more difficult, as many countries specifically require a "state-issued" proof of competence.
- The ministry is creating a **legal vacuum** without a clear benefit for safety on the water.
- The associations fear increased administrative burden and higher costs that could ultimately be passed on to exam candidates.

The DSV has described the planned Sportschifffahrtsverordnung as "unlawful, disproportionate and unsuitable for achieving the government's safety and deregulation goals." The ADAC (Germany's largest automobile and boating association) has also issued its own statement opposing the regulation.

## What does this mean for your SBF preparation?

Regardless of whether the reform is implemented as planned or delayed due to criticism, the core of your exam preparation changes **nothing:**

- The **exam content** (theory, knots, practical) stays the same.
- The official **ELWIS question catalog** remains the foundation.
- Your earned certificate remains **valid indefinitely**.
- The [costs for the Sportbootführerschein](/en/sbf-costs/) may shift slightly over time due to the transition, but are currently stable.

If you are considering getting your Sportbootführerschein, there is no reason to wait. On the contrary: obtaining your license now means you will hold an officially recognized certificate that remains fully valid even after the reform.

## Conclusion

The 2026 Sportbootführerschein reform sounds more dramatic than it actually is for most boaters. Existing licenses remain valid, exam content does not change and the unified 15 HP threshold for all drive types is a sensible simplification. The biggest open question remains how the loss of official government status will affect international recognition.
