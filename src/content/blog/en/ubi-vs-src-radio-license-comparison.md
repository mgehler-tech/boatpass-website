A VHF radio on board is useful, often important for safety, and in many areas simply standard equipment. But operating it legally requires a radio operator's certificate. In Germany, two certificates cover recreational boating: the **UBI** for inland waterways and the **SRC** for coastal waters. They sound similar but cover different areas and are not interchangeable. If you know where you plan to boat, choosing between them is quick.

## The Short Answer

The **UBI (VHF radio certificate for inland shipping, German: UKW-Sprechfunkzeugnis für den Binnenschifffahrtsfunk)** authorizes you to operate a VHF radio on inland waterways such as the Rhine, Elbe, Danube, or canals. The **SRC (Short Range Certificate)** authorizes you to operate a VHF marine radio with DSC in coastal waters (sea area A1, roughly 35 nautical miles from shore). If you only boat on rivers and canals, you need the UBI. If you use VHF radio on the North Sea or Baltic, you need the SRC. If you do both, you eventually need both certificates.

## Why a Radio Certificate at All?

A VHF radio is not a toy. Its channels carry local traffic coordination, lock and bridge announcements, vessel traffic service communication, and, when needed, distress, urgency, and safety calls. Used incorrectly, a radio can block important channels or create confusion exactly when clarity matters most. That is why operating a radio requires an official certificate demonstrating correct procedures, call priorities, and technical basics.

Important to know: both radio certificates are **independent of your boating licence (Sportbootführerschein, SBF)**. The [SBF Inland](https://boatpass.de/sbf-binnen/) or [SBF Coastal](https://boatpass.de/sbf-see/) authorizes you to operate a recreational boat, but says nothing about operating a radio. If you use a VHF set on board, whether fixed or portable, you need the matching radio certificate as well.

## The UBI in Detail

The UBI is examined by the boards of the German Sailing Association (Deutscher Segler-Verband, DSV) and is available from a minimum age of **15**. It is the right certificate if you boat on inland waterways such as the Rhine, Elbe, Danube, or busy canals and want to operate a VHF radio there.

**Exam structure:** The theory exam consists of **22 multiple-choice questions** to be answered in **60 minutes**. You pass with **17 correct answers**. A practical section follows, covering mandatory tasks such as configuring ATIS (the automatic ship identification system for inland waterways), equipment handling, and correctly transmitting distress, urgency, and safety messages.

**Exam content** covers five main areas: traffic circuits on inland waterways, operating procedures and call phrasing, ATIS and equipment knowledge, distress and safety traffic, and radio regulations and frequency use. The official question catalogue contains **130 questions** in total.

**Who specifically needs it?** Beyond boaters on busy inland waterways like the Rhine, Elbe, or Danube, the UBI also applies to anyone using an AIS (Automatic Identification System) unit in transmit mode, as well as charterers of houseboats and larger motorboats on inland waterways.

**Cost:** The official total fee is **€108.71**, made up of admission to the exam (€15.89), the theory and practical exam (€70.03), and issuance of the certificate (€22.79). Retaking the theory or practical part costs €45.64. No preparation course is mandatory, and the theory content can be learned independently.

## The SRC in Detail

The SRC is examined by the boards of the German Sailing Association (DSV) and the German Motor Yacht Association (Deutscher Motoryachtverband, DMYV), is also available from age **15**, is internationally recognized, and does not expire. It becomes mandatory once you operate a VHF marine radio with DSC (Digital Selective Calling) in coastal waters, that is, in **sea area A1** with a range of roughly 35 nautical miles.

**Exam structure:** The theory exam consists of **24 multiple-choice questions** in **30 minutes**, passed with **19 correct answers**. This is combined with a written transcription of a distress, urgency, or safety message, plus a practical section with four tasks on a DSC radio set. An English-language component is also part of the exam: you must receive and translate distress calls and translate German text into English.

**Exam content** covers the VHF marine radio and GMDSS system (Global Maritime Distress and Safety System), DSC procedures, distress, urgency, and safety traffic, ship-to-ship and ship-to-shore communication, NAVTEX, the international phonetic alphabet, distress English, and ITU radio regulations. The exam draws from the official ELWIS question catalogue of **180 questions**.

**Who specifically needs it?** Sailors and motorboaters with a VHF marine radio on board, anyone boating in coastal waters on the North Sea or Baltic, and charterers, since many charter companies require the SRC for yachts equipped with a radio.

**Cost:** The official fee for admission, exam, and certificate issuance is approximately **€127.88**. An optional preparation course at a radio school costs an additional €250 to €350, but is not mandatory.

| Question catalogue | 130 questions | 180 questions |
| Minimum age | 15 | 15 |
| Examining board | DSV | DSV and DMYV |

| Official fee | €108.71 | approx. €127.88 |

Even without the table, the core distinction is clear: inland waterways call for the UBI, coastal waters call for the SRC. The two certificates are **not mutually recognized**: a UBI does not substitute for an SRC, and the reverse is also true.

## What About the LRC?

Anyone planning to use radio beyond VHF range, for example on long-distance passages using medium-wave, short-wave, or satellite communication, needs the additional **LRC (Long Range Certificate)**. The SRC only covers VHF operation in coastal waters. For the vast majority of recreational boaters on the Baltic or North Sea, the SRC is entirely sufficient.

## Practical Tip: Combining Both Certificates

If you boat both on inland waterways and at sea, you will eventually need both certificates. There is a useful shortcut: holders of a maritime radio certificate such as the SRC or LRC get a shortened theoretical and practical UBI exam. In many cases, both exams can even be taken on the same day. If you are planning to get both certificates anyway, it can make sense to get the SRC first and then take the shortened UBI exam afterward.

## Do I Even Need a Radio Certificate?

That depends on your cruising area and your boat's equipment. A boat without a VHF radio needs neither certificate. But as soon as a VHF radio is used on board, whether built in or portable, the matching radio certificate is mandatory, regardless of whether you personally operate it or someone else on board does. Anyone using an AIS unit in transmit mode generally also needs the UBI or an equivalent radio certificate.

Many charter companies require the SRC or UBI outright if the charter boat is fitted with a radio set. It is worth clarifying this before booking so your trip planning does not run into last-minute surprises.

## Typical Real-World Scenarios

Sometimes a concrete example helps more than an abstract rule. A few typical situations:

- **Motorboat with a radio on the Rhine or Elbe:** The UBI is sufficient here. As long as you only cruise inland waterways, you do not need the SRC.
- **Sailing yacht on the Baltic with a VHF marine radio:** The SRC is mandatory here. Even if you occasionally pass through a canal or river mouth, coastal sea area rules apply for marine radio use.
- **Charter boat on Lake Constance that uses the Rhine as an approach and also sails the open lake:** Depending on equipment and charter conditions, both certificates can become relevant. It is best to clarify this directly with the charter company.
- **Houseboat on canals using AIS in transmit mode:** Even without a conventional radio set, the UBI can be required here as soon as the AIS unit transmits rather than just receives.
- **Long-distance passage using short-wave radio beyond coastal range:** The SRC is no longer sufficient here; the LRC is required.

If in doubt, it is better to hold more certificates than fewer. An extra certificate never hurts, while a missing one can become costly if it comes up during a waterway police inspection.

## How to Prepare

No preparation course is mandatory for either exam. The official question catalogues, 130 questions for the UBI and 180 for the SRC, are freely available and can be studied independently. If you do not want to underestimate the practical section, plan some hands-on practice with the radio set beforehand, especially for ATIS configuration on the UBI or DSC operation on the SRC.

For the theory portion, structured practice with realistic exam simulation is far more effective than memorizing individual questions, since the actual exam often rephrases questions or presents them in a different order than the catalogue.

## Conclusion

The UBI and SRC answer the same underlying question from two different angles: where do you want to use your radio? On inland waterways such as the Rhine, Elbe, or Danube, you need the UBI, with its 22 theory questions in 60 minutes. In coastal waters on the North Sea and Baltic, the SRC, with 24 questions in 30 minutes plus practical DSC operation, is the right choice. Both certificates are independent of the boating licence, independent of each other, and not mutually recognized, though holders of an SRC or LRC benefit from a shortened UBI exam. If you cruise both types of waters, plan for both certificates in the long run.
