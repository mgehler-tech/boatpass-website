The exam is long behind you, the season is in full swing, and then it happens: your Sportbootführerschein (German recreational boating license) is gone, soaked through in your wallet, or lost somewhere during a move. No reason to panic, but it is time to act. This article explains where to request a replacement, which documents you need, and what to expect from the process.

## Who issues the replacement?

Your [Sportbootführerschein](/en/sbf-inland/) was originally issued by one of two associations:

- **DSV** – Deutscher Segler-Verband (German Sailing Association)
- **DMYV** – Deutscher Motoryachtverband (German Motor Yacht Association)

Both are commissioned by the Federal Ministry of Transport to administer the SBF exams and issue the licenses, as described in the article on [registering for the SBF exam](/en/blog/how-to-register-sbf-exam/). For a replacement, you contact the specific association that examined you and issued your original license.

If you no longer remember exactly which association that was, that is not a major problem: the two associations work closely together. If you reach the wrong one, you will typically just be forwarded to the right one. Still, it helps if you roughly remember when and where you took the exam, since that speeds up the search for your old records.

Good to know: what matters for jurisdiction is which association originally issued your license, not your current address. If you have moved since your exam, or now live in a different German state, that does not change which association you need to contact for a replacement.

## Lost, stolen, or damaged: does it make a difference?

The basic process for a replacement is similar in all three cases, but there are a few small, important differences.

### License lost

For a simple loss, for example if the license is no longer to be found at home or on the boat, you submit the replacement application directly to the responsible association. A police report is generally not required in this case.

### License stolen

If your license was stolen, for example along with your wallet, you should report it to the police and **file a report**. Attach a copy of that police report to your replacement application. Doing so is also in your own interest, in case the license is later misused in connection with other offenses.

### License damaged

If your license has become illegible or is no longer clearly recognizable as an official document, for example due to water damage, heat, or mechanical damage, you enclose the damaged original with your application. As long as the license is still clearly legible and the relevant details (name, date of birth, license type, issuing association) are clearly recognizable, it generally remains valid. If the damage is more severe, do not wait until a check raises questions; apply for a replacement promptly instead.

## What documents do you need for the application?

The exact forms differ slightly between DSV and DMYV, but at their core you will typically need:

- **A completed replacement application form**, downloadable from the website of the responsible association
- **Details of your original license:** license type (SBF Inland, SBF Coastal, or both), and as precise as possible information on when and where you took the exam
- **A current passport photo** in the usual biometric format, similar to what you submitted for your original application
- **A copy of a valid ID document**, showing your name, date of birth, place of birth, and address clearly
- For **theft:** a copy of the police report
- For **damage:** the damaged original license
- Details for **paying the processing fee**, depending on the association either by bank transfer or direct debit

Before filling anything out, it is worth checking the association's website for the current version of the form and whether any additional information is required, since forms and processes occasionally change.

## What if you cannot remember all the details?

Knowing the exact exam date helps, but is not always strictly required. These clues are often useful:

- Invoices or payment receipts from your boating school or exam registration at the time
- Confirmation emails from around when you took the exam
- The approximate period (year, possibly season) in which you took the exam

The more precise your information, the faster the association can locate your original record in the license register and issue the replacement.

## How long does processing take?

Realistically, plan for **several weeks**, with processing often taking around three to five weeks. During peak season, when many applications arrive at once, it can take longer. If you need your license on short notice, for example because a charter trip is coming up, apply as early as possible rather than shortly before your planned trip.

Some associations offer, for an additional fee, a **temporary proof of license** to bridge the waiting period until the final replacement arrives. Whether and in what form this is offered can change, so check directly with the responsible association.

## Cost of a replacement

A **processing fee** applies, and the exact amount is best checked directly against the current fee schedule on the responsible association's website, since fees can change. Additional costs may apply for postage or an optional temporary proof of license. You can find a general overview of costs related to the Sportbootführerschein on our [costs page](/en/sbf-costs/).

Compared to the cost of the original exam, the fee for a replacement is significantly lower, since you do not need to retake either the theory or the practical test. The real effort lies less in the money and more in the time you should plan for gathering documents and waiting for processing.

## Requesting a replacement while abroad

If you lose your license during a trip abroad, for example on a charter boat in Croatia or the Netherlands, the situation becomes more uncomfortable, because many countries require you to carry a valid license on board. For details on where the Sportbootführerschein is actually recognized abroad, see the article [Is the SBF Valid Abroad?](/en/blog/sbf-valid-abroad/). In practice this means: sort out a replacement **before** a trip abroad if you already know your license is damaged or in poor condition. If it goes missing during the trip, at least inform the charter company or crew, document the loss (and, in case of theft, file a report with the local police), and apply for a replacement once you are back in Germany.

## A practical example

Imagine you are packing your gear for a weekend trip on a Friday evening and notice your Sportbootführerschein is missing from your document folder. It probably went missing somewhere between the car and the jetty during one of your last check-ins. Instead of risking the trip or spending days searching, you take a structured approach: you check whether you have a copy or photo of the license, note down the exam year and location as precisely as you can, and look up the current application form from the responsible association online. That same evening, you fill out the application, attach a passport photo and a copy of your ID, and send everything off. It will not help for the upcoming trip, but you have set the process in motion instead of putting it off for weeks, and the next season starts with valid paperwork.

## Prevention: how to avoid the hassle in the future

You can never fully rule out loss or damage, but a few simple habits reduce the risk considerably:

- **Keep a copy at home.** A simple photocopy or scan does not replace the original, but it helps with the application, since you have the license type, issue date, and association in black and white.
- **Make a digital backup.** A photo of the license on your phone, ideally also backed up to the cloud, takes seconds and is worth a lot if you need to recall details.
- **Use a waterproof document folder on board.** Moisture is one of the most common causes of damaged paperwork on the water. A simple waterproof sleeve or folder reliably protects your license, boat registration, and other documents.
- **Do not carry the license around in your wallet all the time.** Carrying it with you constantly increases the risk of losing it or having it stolen along with your other valuables. Bring it along specifically when you actually need it on board.

## Replacement vs. reissue: do not confuse the two

Besides a replacement for a lost or damaged license, DSV and DMYV also offer a **reissue** (Umschreibung), for example if you want to exchange an old paper license for the current credit-card format, or if your name has changed. That is a separate process with partly different documents. If your license still exists but is outdated or issued under an old name, ask the association specifically about a **reissue**, not a replacement.

## Step by step

1. **Identify the association:** DSV or DMYV, depending on where you took your exam.
2. **Classify your case:** loss, theft, or damage, each with the matching documents.
3. **Download the form** from the association's current website and fill it out completely.
4. **Gather your documents:** passport photo, ID copy, and, if applicable, a police report or the damaged original.
5. **Submit the application** and pay the processing fee as instructed by the association.
6. **Plan ahead**, especially before a planned trip or charter, since processing can take several weeks.

## Conclusion

A lost or damaged Sportbootführerschein is annoying, but not a disaster: through the responsible association, DSV or DMYV, requesting a replacement is a straightforward process. What matters is complete documentation, a police report in case of theft, and above all enough lead time, since processing can take several weeks.
